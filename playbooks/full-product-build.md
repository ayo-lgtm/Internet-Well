# Playbook: Full Product Build — Idea to Launch

## Purpose

Take a product from a founder's outcome through validated scope, architecture, implementation, launch, and operating readiness using the smallest defensible set of capabilities and resources.

## Inputs

Founder goal, target users, problem evidence, constraints, jurisdictions, budget, timeline, existing assets, preferred stack, business model, risk tolerance, and authority.

## Workflow

1. Define user, problem, promised outcome, critical journey, exclusions, and success evidence.
2. Validate the problem and alternatives before expanding scope.
3. Create project assessment, risk class, product profile, and stack profile.
4. Design architecture, data flows, trust boundaries, decisions, and rollback.
5. Select the smallest resource bundle for product, engineering, testing, security, privacy, accessibility, legal, AI, operations, marketing, and finance needs.
6. Build thin vertical slices; verify each through public behavior.
7. Establish CI, evaluations, monitoring, backups, incident response, support, and documentation.
8. Run existing-product audit and launch-readiness playbooks.
9. Resolve blockers, obtain human reviews, and make an explicit launch decision.
10. Launch only with authorization; measure activation, value, reliability, harm, cost, retention, and learning.
11. Maintain continuous verification and recheck upstream resources.

## Outputs

Product requirements, assessment, architecture, decisions, capability bundle, implementation plan, verified increments, launch report, operating system, marketing plan, metrics, and post-launch learning plan.

## Verification

Map every acceptance criterion to fresh evidence; test critical journeys, failures, access, data, security, privacy, accessibility, AI behavior, deployment, rollback, backup restore, monitoring, and support. Distinguish passed, failed, blocked, and unverified.

## Stop conditions

Stop before building unsupported scope, copying upstream repos, installing unreviewed high-risk dependencies, processing sensitive data through new providers, deploying, purchasing, publishing, or launching without authorization and required reviews.

## Human review

Qualified product, engineering, security, privacy, legal, finance, accessibility, domain, and operations reviewers must approve material high-risk decisions. Internet-Well reduces mistakes; it does not replace specialists or guarantee product-market fit, security, compliance, profit, or a bug-free launch.
